\begin{abstract}
Public and media controversies across European football routinely cite a club's ratio of yellow cards to fouls committed as evidence of refereeing favouritism or tactical impunity. Across a four-study empirical programme spanning $58,013$ completed-season matches ($115,984$ team-matches and $246$ clubs with $\ge 150$ matches) in $11$ European top divisions ($2000\text{--}2025$), $35,844$ referee-tracked team-matches across $392$ officials in England, Scotland, and Portugal, $47,955$ spatio-temporally coded fouls from five major leagues, and $3,100$ historical four-match season openings, we show that raw cards-per-foul ratios are systematically confounded at four levels. First, an audit of $286$ league-season files shows that $107$ files return valid tables with zero match statistics while referee identifiers are absent in $9$ of $11$ leagues. Second, under naive proportional ratios ($I^{\text{raw}}_i$), club strength correlates negatively with booking rates across Europe ($r = -0.371, p = 1.9\times 10^{-9}$), making nearly every domestic hegemon---Olympiacos ($0.769$), Celtic ($0.828$), Liverpool ($0.876$), Club Brugge ($0.878$), SL Benfica ($0.885$), FC Porto ($0.890$), Juventus ($0.907$), Feyenoord ($0.919$), Borussia Dortmund ($0.920$), Bayern Munich ($0.940$), Inter ($0.944$), PSV ($0.945$), Ajax ($0.954$), Barcelona ($0.967$), and Real Madrid ($0.977$)---appear leniently officiated. This stems from pre-match win expectation ($1.098\times$ heavy underdog vs.\ $0.856\times$ heavy favourite median across $11$ leagues) and non-foul conduct intercepts ($11.1\%\text{--}38.8\%$ league-mean card share; $0.197$ yellows/foul at $\le 6$ fouls vs.\ $0.122$ at $>20$ fouls). Crucially, testing each club's \emph{opponents} reveals that team strength raises the opponent's booking index ($r = +0.283, p = 6.6\times 10^{-6}$) and that holding club identity fixed across opponent-strength terciles raises booking rates in $11$ of $11$ leagues ($0.938 \to 0.993 \to 1.088$). Once both the affine intercept and fixture quality are netted out ($I^{\text{fix}}_i$), the strength correlation vanishes ($r = +0.095, p = 0.139$), top and bottom strength quartiles equalize ($1.012$ vs.\ $1.013, p = 0.917$), and Europe's perennial champions collapse onto parity ($\approx 1.00$), while leave-one-club-out referee adjustment across $80$ English, Scottish, and Portuguese clubs shifts indices by an RMS of just $0.0085$ ($r = +0.990$). Third, at the event level ($N = 47,955$), card risk drops $3.5$-fold across the pitch ($31.4\%$ to $8.9\%$), but clubs' mean foul locations compress into $[48.2, 58.0]$, sitting inside a matched spatial-Poisson null ($\bar{r} = -0.210, [-0.397, -0.015]$). Fourth, across $2,528$ consecutive club-season pairs, $84.9\%$ of single-season variance is Poisson noise, Empirical-Bayes shrinkage reduces out-of-sample RMSE by $13.3\%$, and extreme early-season openings across all $11$ leagues revert toward parity ($0.965$ median rest-of-season index).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In professional association football, disciplinary statistics---fouls committed and yellow or red cards issued---are the most widely available behavioural records outside goals and shots. Because raw counts of fouls and bookings are published for almost every modern top-flight match, analysts, club officials, and supporters across Europe routinely construct a simple ratio: yellow cards per foul committed (or its reciprocal, fouls per card). Whenever a dominant domestic club accumulates a low ratio of cards to fouls, the discrepancy is interpreted either as institutional favouritism by match officials or as a deliberate tactical skill: committing fouls in pitch zones or match states where referees are reluctant to book \citep{phatak2021dirty,azmat2024xb}.

Evaluating this claim across the entire $11$-league European archive ($58,013$ completed matches, $115,984$ team-matches, and $246$ clubs with $\ge 150$ matches) reveals a striking stylized fact: under a naive proportional booking index $I^{\text{raw}}_i$, club strength correlates strongly and negatively with cards per foul ($r = -0.371, p = 1.9\times 10^{-9}$). Nearly every perennial domestic champion in Europe---including Olympiacos ($0.769$), Celtic ($0.828$), Liverpool ($0.876$), Club Brugge ($0.878$), SL Benfica ($0.885$), FC Porto ($0.890$), Juventus ($0.907$), Feyenoord ($0.919$), Borussia Dortmund ($0.920$), Rangers ($0.939$), Bayern Munich ($0.940$), Inter ($0.944$), PSV Eindhoven ($0.945$), Ajax ($0.954$), Barcelona ($0.967$), Galatasaray ($0.975$), and Real Madrid ($0.977$)---sits below parity ($1.000$). This paper demonstrates that reasoning from raw disciplinary ratios to club-level officiating or behavioural claims commits four sequential methodological errors:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Silent schema and denominator traps (Study 01):} Public football repositories exhibit severe longitudinal truncation, unrecorded rule differences in second-yellow accounting, and missing referee identifiers across $9$ of $11$ European leagues.
  \item \textbf{Proportional misspecification and fixture-level confounding (Study 02):} Yellow cards are an affine rather than proportional function of fouls because conduct offences (dissent, delaying restarts, simulation) enter as a positive intercept ($11.1\%\text{--}38.8\%$ league-mean share; $13\%\text{--}47\%$ across league-seasons). Adjusting for pre-match win expectation removes the favourite discount ($1.098\times$ vs.\ $0.856\times$) but overshoots for elite clubs ($r = +0.484$) unless fixture quality is also accounted for: testing each club's \emph{opponents} proves that high-quality fixtures elevate card-per-foul rates for \emph{both} teams on the pitch ($r = +0.283$; $11/11$ leagues rising across opponent-strength terciles). Netting out both the affine intercept and fixture quality ($I^{\text{fix}}_i$) eliminates the strength gradient across all $246$ clubs ($r = +0.095, p = 0.139$) and pulls Europe's domestic champions to parity ($\approx 1.00$).
  \item \textbf{Event-to-actor ecological compression (Study 03):} Although the spatial and temporal card gradient at the level of an individual foul is steep ($31.4\%$ in the defensive fifth vs.\ $8.9\%$ in the attacking fifth; $25.7\%$ in late-game close-score defensive/middle-third situations vs.\ $14.2\%$ outside), clubs stand in nearly the same place on the pitch ($[48.2, 58.0]$ mean $x$-coordinate), leaving $91.2\%$ of between-club variation unexplained even on the most favourable tail statistic.
  \item \textbf{Small-sample Poisson dominance (Study 04 \& Cross-European Scan):} Across $2,528$ consecutive club-season pairs in $11$ leagues, $84.9\%$ of single-season variance in the adjusted booking index is Poisson noise. Consequently, a club's own one-season raw ratio is outperformed as an out-of-sample predictor of its next season by $11.6\%$ simply by predicting the league mean, and by $13.3\%$ via Empirical-Bayes shrinkage \citep{efron1975stein,brown2008season}.
\end{enumerate}

Every empirical claim in this paper is bound by continuous-integration tests to deterministic JSON sidecars and Parquet snapshots committed in an open repository, accompanied by a six-attack adversarial pressure-test matrix.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Referee bias, social pressure, and team strength.} A rich econometric and sports-science literature examines officiating in football. \citet{garicano2005favoritism} and \citet{dohmen2008social} established that referees adjust injury time in response to score state and crowd pressure, while \citet{boyko2007referee} and \citet{sors2021silence} documented home-crowd effects on disciplinary sanctions that attenuated in spectator-free matches. Most relevant to our Study~02, \citet{dawson2007referees} analysed $2,660$ English Premier League matches ($1996\text{--}2003$) using a bivariate Negative Binomial and Nash-equilibrium aggression model, finding significant referee-to-referee inconsistency alongside a team-strength gradient in total bookings. We extend \citet{dawson2007referees} to $58,013$ matches and $246$ clubs across $11$ leagues while conditioning explicitly on foul volume, decomposing own versus opponent indices within the same fixture, and performing leave-one-club-out referee decomposition across $35,844$ team-matches in England, Scotland, and Portugal.

\textbf{Fouling incentives, spatial risk, and Simpson's paradox.} \citet{wright2003professional} modelled when a professional foul is tactically rational, and \citet{ridder1994down} estimated the scoring cost of red-card dismissals. \citet{phatak2021dirty} compared fouls-per-card across five European leagues and demonstrated Simpson's paradox \citep{simpson1951interpretation} when pooling across leagues with distinct officiating baselines. At the individual event level, \citet{pappalardo2019events} released spatio-temporal event logs for the $2017\text{--}18$ Big-Five European seasons, and \citet{azmat2024xb} formulated an Expected Booking ($\text{xB}$) model of $P(\text{card}\mid\text{foul context})$. While \citet{azmat2024xb} establish that event context predicts whether \emph{a} foul is carded, our Study~03 tests whether real clubs actually separate along that gradient.

\textbf{Reliability, shrinkage, and false discovery.} Separating persistent skill from random noise in sports metrics relies on split-half reliability \citep{cronbach1951alpha}, Empirical-Bayes shrinkage \citep{efron1975stein,brown2008season}, partial pooling \citep{gelman2012multiple}, and False Discovery Rate (FDR) control \citep{benjamini1995controlling}, alongside guards against regression-to-the-mean artifacts \citep{barnett2005regression} and undisclosed researcher degrees of freedom \citep{simmons2011false,ioannidis2005why,peng2011reproducible}. \citet{johnson2010stable} raised the profile-stabilisation question for football match indicators; our Study~04 provides the exact variance decomposition and out-of-sample shrinkage validation for disciplinary indices across $11$ leagues.

Table~\ref{tab:study01} summarises four structural findings that constrain any valid multi-league disciplinary study:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Silent absence of match statistics:} Only $179$ of $286$ league-season files ($62.6\%$) contain foul and card columns ($58,013$ completed matches; $190$ files when including the in-progress $2026\text{--}27$ snapshot). The remaining $107$ files download with HTTP~$200$ and parse cleanly into DataFrames containing only scores and betting odds. In Portugal, Netherlands, and Turkey, foul and card records begin only in $2017\text{--}18$; in Belgium and Greece, in $2019\text{--}20$.
  \item \textbf{Interrupted series:} Germany carries fouls, cards, and referee identities in $2000\text{--}01$ and $2001\text{--}02$ ($1,222$ team-matches, $77$ referees), drops all match statistics in $2002\text{--}03$, and resumes fouls and cards (without referees) from $2003\text{--}04$.
  \item \textbf{Referee coverage contraction:} Match-official identity is recorded continuously only in England ($19,580$ team-matches, $181$ referees) and Scotland ($10,756$ team-matches, $160$ referees, bar $2012\text{--}13$), for two seasons each in Germany and Italy ($1,500$ team-matches, $47$ referees), and never in the other seven leagues.
  \item \textbf{Schema widening and definitional traps:} While median file width quintupled from $25$ columns ($2000\text{--}01$) to $131$ columns ($2025\text{--}26$) due to bookmaker odds proliferation, pitch-event columns rose from $0$ to $12$ by $2007$ and remained frozen thereafter. Moreover, two definitional traps prohibit unstratified cross-league pooling: English and Scottish feeds exclude the first yellow card of a second-bookable dismissal whereas continental feeds count both, and match-level yellow-card totals include non-foul bookings (dissent, time-wasting, bench sanctions).
\end{enumerate}

To test whether clubs across Europe exhibit anomalous booking rates relative to the fouls they commit, we analysed all $58,013$ completed matches ($115,984$ team-matches with pre-match odds/Elo strength; $115,946$ with $>0$ fouls) and all $246$ clubs with $\ge 150$ team-matches across the $11$ European top divisions.

\subsection{The Naive Estimator and Its Four Failures Across Europe}
Let $Y_{i,m}$ and $F_{i,m}$ denote the yellow cards received and fouls committed by team $i$ in match $m$. Under the standard proportional model $E_{\text{raw}}[Y_{i,m}] = \bar{r}_{L,s} F_{i,m}$ (where $\bar{r}_{L,s} = \sum Y / \sum F$ within league $L$ and season $s$), a club's raw booking index is $I^{\text{raw}}_i = \sum_m Y_{i,m} / \sum_m E_{\text{raw}}[Y_{i,m}]$. Across the $246$-club European universe, $I^{\text{raw}}_i$ correlates negatively with club strength $s_i$ at $r = \mathbf{-0.371}$ ($p = 1.9\times 10^{-9}$): the top strength quartile ($N = 62$ clubs) averages $0.971$ versus $1.026$ for the bottom quartile ($p = 1.0\times 10^{-6}$), and the $26$ elite domestic title contenders ($s_i \ge 1.0$) average $0.949$ (median $0.944$). Four sequential diagnostics demonstrate why this apparent leniency toward dominant clubs is a statistical artifact.

\textbf{Failure 1: Pre-match strength and match expectation.} Using devigged pre-match bookmaker odds to compute each side's log-odds strength differential $s_{i,m}$, Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:league_universe} partition all $115,984$ team-matches into five expectation bands. Across all $11$ European leagues, heavy underdogs are booked at a higher rate per foul than heavy favourites in $\mathbf{11}$ of $\mathbf{11}$ competitions (median $\mathbf{1.098\times}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{0.856\times}$; strictly monotone across all five bands in $7$ of $11$ leagues, spanning from $0.907 \to 1.038$ in Spain to $0.802 \to 1.157$ in Scotland and $0.778 \to 1.158$ in Greece). Because domestic champions are heavy favourites in most league fixtures, attributing the favourite discount to club-specific referee bias confounds club identity with match role.

\textbf{Failure 2: Affine misspecification and non-foul intercepts.} The proportional model assumes zero cards at zero fouls ($a = 0$). Fitting the affine specification $E_{\text{aff}}[Y_{i,m}\mid F_{i,m}] = a_{L,s} + b_{L,s} F_{i,m}$ within each league-season reveals a positive intercept $a_{L,s}$ accounting for $11.1\%$ (England) to $38.8\%$ (Greece) of league-mean yellow cards (Table~\ref{tab:league_universe}; spanning $13\%\text{--}47\%$ across individual league-seasons). Figure~\ref{fig:fig2}(a) shows the empirical consequence: observed yellows per foul $Y/F = a/F + b$ declines monotonically with foul volume across all nine bins, from $0.197$ when a side commits $\le 6$ fouls ($5.1$ bin mean) to $0.122$ when it commits $>20$ fouls ($23.4$ bin mean). Cross-checking against the Wyscout event-coded sub-types in our Study~03 dataset (\texttt{typology.json}) confirms the physical mechanism: four non-challenge conduct labels (\emph{Protest}, \emph{Time lost foul}, \emph{Simulation}, and \emph{Out of game foul}) produce $642$ of the $7,346$ total cards ($8.74\%$ of all cards, carded at $99.1\%$), with individual club non-play card shares ranging from $1.5\%$ to $17.9\%$ (median $8.1\%$, split-half reliability $r = 0.251$, Spearman--Brown corrected $0.402$).

\textbf{Failure 3: The opponent test and fixture-level confounding.} When expectation is adjusted for pre-match strength differential $s_{i,m}$ alone (via five role bands $I^{\text{sit}}_i$ or a continuous quartic Poisson log-offset polynomial $I^{\text{quart}}_i$), the correlation with club strength flips sign across the $246$ clubs ($r = +0.403$ for band-adjusted; $r = +0.484, p = 7.8\times 10^{-16}$, Spearman $\rho = +0.349$ for continuous quartic, positive in $11$ of $11$ leagues). Why do elite clubs appear to be booked \emph{more} than expected once single-match win probability is controlled for? Computing the identical adjusted index for each club's \emph{opponents} in the matches played against that club yields $r = \mathbf{+0.283}$ ($p = 6.6\times 10^{-6}$), while a club's own index and its opponents' index are nearly independent ($r = +0.116, p = 0.069$). Holding club identity fixed and partitioning each club's matches into terciles of opponent season strength shows that a team's booking index rises with the quality of the opponent it faces in $\mathbf{11}$ of $\mathbf{11}$ leagues (Table~\ref{tab:league_universe}): across the $11$-league median, the same club is booked at $\mathbf{0.938}$ against weakest-tercile opponents, $\mathbf{0.993}$ against middle-tercile opponents, and $\mathbf{1.088}$ against strongest-tercile opponents. Thus, yellow cards per foul scale with the competitive intensity of the \emph{fixture} for both teams.

Incorporating both clubs' season strengths $(\bar{s}_{i,s}, \bar{s}_{j,s})$ alongside match strength differential $s_{i,m}$ on top of the affine baseline ($I^{\text{fix}}_i$) resolves the over-correction completely: across all $246$ European clubs, $\text{corr}(s_i, I^{\text{fix}}_i)$ drops to $\mathbf{+0.095}$ ($p = \mathbf{0.139}$), and the top and bottom strength quartiles become indistinguishable ($\mathbf{1.012}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{1.013}$, two-sample $t$-test $p = \mathbf{0.917}$). Testing whether residual club variation belongs to the head coach across $42$ multi-club managerial spell pairs yields a null correlation of $r = -0.076$ ($5,000$-permutation $p = 0.628$).

\textbf{Failure 4: Ecological reversal across aggregation levels.} Across the $11$ league averages, the steepness of a league's strength gradient correlates with its mean home goal difference at $r = +0.782$ ($p = 0.0045$). Yet when the same panel is disaggregated into $62$ three-season blocks, Figure~\ref{fig:fig2}(b) shows the correlation dropping to $r = +0.238$ ($p = 0.063$) when pooled and reversing to $r = -0.086$ ($p = 0.504$, negative in $5$ of $6$ leagues with $\ge 4$ blocks) within leagues over time, despite home advantage falling from $0.349$ goals/match in $2001$ to $0.220$ in $2025$.

\subsection{Multi-League Referee Decomposition and Universe Deconfounding}
Could apparent club anomalies in leagues with referee tracking arise from biased official assignments? We executed a leave-one-club-out referee decomposition across all three multi-season referee-tracked leagues---England (\texttt{E0}, $19,580$ team-matches, $36$ clubs), Scotland (\texttt{SC0}, $10,756$ team-matches, $18$ clubs), and Portugal (\texttt{P1}, $5,508$ team-matches joined to $2,737$ harvested official appointments, $26$ clubs with $\ge 80$ matches)---totalling $\mathbf{35,844}$ team-matches, $\mathbf{392}$ referees, $\mathbf{144}$ officials with $\ge 40$ matches, and $\mathbf{80}$ clubs (Table~\ref{tab:league_universe}).

Estimating each official's strictness multiplier $\hat{\rho}_{r,-i}$ \emph{excluding} all matches involving club $i$ reveals two universal structural properties:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Wide individual official variance:} Referee strictness multipliers $\hat{\rho}_r$ span $\mathbf{0.593\times}$ to $\mathbf{1.193\times}$ in England (spread $0.600$), $\mathbf{0.662\times}$ to $\mathbf{1.206\times}$ in Scotland (spread $0.544$), and $\mathbf{0.755\times}$ to $\mathbf{1.324\times}$ in Portugal (spread $0.569$)---comparable in magnitude to the pre-match role effect.
  \item \textbf{Balanced long-run club assignment:} Across all $80$ clubs in England, Scotland, and Portugal, every club's leave-one-club-out mean referee draw $\bar{\rho}_{-i}$ falls within the narrow interval $\mathbf{[0.971, 1.021]}$ ($[0.979, 1.019]$ in \texttt{E0}, $[0.996, 1.013]$ in \texttt{SC0}, $[0.971, 1.021]$ in \texttt{P1}). Consequently, referee assignment moves club booking indices by a root-mean-square shift of only $\mathbf{0.0085}$ ($0.007$ in \texttt{E0}, $0.006$ in \texttt{SC0}, $0.011$ in \texttt{P1}), with a correlation of $\mathbf{r = +0.990}$ between situation-adjusted and referee-adjusted indices across the $80$ clubs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Study 03: The Spatial and Temporal Lever Nobody Pulls}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig3.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Study 03: Event-level disciplinary gradients vs.\ club-level spatial compression ($N = 47,955$ fouls, $1,826$ matches, $98$ Big-Five clubs).} \textbf{(a)} Card probability per foul falls $3.5$-fold from a team's own defensive fifth ($31.4\%$) to the attacking fifth ($8.9\%$), but all $98$ clubs' season-mean foul $x$-coordinates compress into $[48.2, 58.0]$ (amber band), yielding $r = -0.143$ ($[-0.332, +0.057]$), indistinguishable from a $2,000$-draw matched spatial-Poisson null ($\bar{r} = -0.210, [-0.397, -0.015]$). \textbf{(b)} Temporal escalation from $6.5\%$ ($0\text{--}15'$) to $25.6\%$ ($90+'$), featuring a significant $-3.3\text{ pp}$ half-time reset ($16.1\% \to 12.8\%, p = 4.1\times 10^{-4}$) and a $25.7\%$ rate inside the late-game ($\ge 70'$), close-score ($0, +1$) defensive/middle-third tactical window ($R^2 = 0.3\%$ between clubs).}
\label{fig:fig3}
\end{figure*}

Could a club achieve a genuinely low card-per-foul rate by committing its fouls higher up the pitch or at safer moments? To test this mechanism at the event level, we analysed all $47,955$ fouls across $1,826$ matches and $98$ clubs ($\ge 200$ fouls each) in the $2017\text{--}18$ English, French, German, Italian, and Spanish top divisions from \citet{pappalardo2019events} (overall base card rate $15.32\%$).

\subsection{Steep Event Gradients and the Half-Time Discontinuity}
At the level of an individual foul, pitch location, elapsed time, and score state exert massive effects on card probability (Figure~\ref{fig:fig3}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Longitudinal and lateral location:} A foul in the fouling team's own defensive fifth ($x \le 20$) draws a card $\mathbf{31.4\%}$ of the time, compared with $\mathbf{8.9\%}$ in the attacking fifth ($x > 80$)---a $3.54\times$ ratio (ranging from $2.47\times$ in England to $4.97\times$ in Germany). By pitch thirds, card risk is $24.1\%$ in the defensive third, $14.0\%$ in the middle third, and $10.6\%$ in the attacking third ($22.8\%$, $12.7\%$, and $9.3\%$ when non-play conduct cards are removed). Central fouls ($|y - 50| \le 10$) are carded at $19.0\%$ versus $13.0\%$ wide ($|y - 50| > 40$).
  \item \textbf{Elapsed time and the half-time reset:} Card probability quadruples from $\mathbf{6.5\%}$ in the opening quarter-hour ($0\text{--}15'$) to $\mathbf{25.6\%}$ in second-half stoppage time ($>90'$). Crucially, the trajectory is not monotonic: card rate climbs to $\mathbf{16.1\%}$ in the final five minutes of the first half ($40\text{--}45'$), then drops by $\mathbf{3.3}$ percentage points to $\mathbf{12.8\%}$ in the opening five minutes of the second half ($45\text{--}50'$, $\chi^2$ contingency $p = 4.1\times 10^{-4}$). Because physiological fatigue does not discharge to first-half levels across a fifteen-minute interval, this discontinuity reflects within-half referee tolerance accumulation and reset.
  \item \textbf{Score state:} Fouls committed while trailing are carded at $\mathbf{18.1\%}$, while leading at $16.2\%$, and while level at $\mathbf{13.3\%}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why the Event Gradient Vanishes Between Clubs}
Despite a $3.5$-fold spatial gradient across the $100$-unit pitch, the season-average foul position across all $98$ Big-Five clubs spans only $\mathbf{48.2}$ to $\mathbf{58.0}$---less than one-tenth of the pitch (Figure~\ref{fig:fig3}(a)). Using \citet{cronbach1951alpha} split-variance reliability to net out within-club sampling error, $70.1\%$ of the between-club variance in mean foul $x$-position is genuine tactical variation (versus only $13.7\%$ for mean foul minute), and shifting from the worst to the best observed spatial-temporal foul mix among the $98$ clubs is worth $17.9\%$ of the base card rate ($16.6\%$ vs.\ $13.9\%$, and at most a $7.3\%$ reduction below a league's base rate in the most generous league, Spain).

Across the $98$ clubs, mean foul $x$-coordinate correlates with club card rate at $r = -0.143$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.332, +0.057], p = 0.160$; $80\%$ power threshold $|r| = 0.28$), and an additive logistic regression in $(x, x^2, |y-50|, \text{minute}, \text{score state})$ explains only $\mathbf{3.1\%}$ of the between-club standard deviation in cards per foul.

\textbf{Matched spatial-Poisson and permutation nulls.} To separate ``no club exploits the spatial gradient'' from ``the observed $[48.2, 58.0]$ placement range is too narrow to overcome single-season Poisson noise,'' we executed two $2,000$-draw Monte Carlo nulls. Under a spatial-permutation null that randomly reassigns club mean $x$-positions across the $98$ clubs, $\bar{r} = +0.002$ ($95\%$ interval $[-0.193, +0.199]$, two-sided $p = 0.161$). Under a matched spatial-Poisson null where each club's actual foul coordinates in $[48.2, 58.0]$ are held fixed and club card totals are drawn from $\text{Poisson}(\sum_{j \in c} \hat{p}_{\text{spatial}}(x_j, y_j))$, the expected correlation is $\bar{r} = \mathbf{-0.210}$ with a $95\%$ sampling interval of $\mathbf{[-0.397, -0.015]}$. The observed $r = -0.143$ sits comfortably inside \emph{both} distributions: because clubs compress into a $9.8$-unit band near midfield, the mechanical slope induced by the spatial curve is smaller than the Poisson sampling noise of a $38$-match season.

\textbf{Tail-share versus volume proxy.} Replacing mean foul $x$ with a tail statistic---the share of a club's fouls committed in its own defensive third ($x < 33.3$, spanning $16.1\%$ to $29.9\%$ across the $98$ clubs)---strengthens the club-level correlation to $r = \mathbf{+0.296}$ ($95\%$ CI $[+0.103, +0.467]$, $R^2 = \mathbf{8.8\%}$; $r = +0.305, R^2 = 9.3\%$ on play-only fouls). Conversely, expressing defensive-third fouls as a per-match count ($1.76$ to $4.32$, median $3.07$) correlates with cards per match at $r = +0.670$ ($[+0.544, +0.766]$) solely because total fouls per match correlates with cards per match at $r = +0.696$ ($[+0.577, +0.786]$).

\textbf{Situational tactical-fouling interaction test.} Isolating high-leverage fouls---late in the match ($\text{minute} \ge 70$), in a close game ($\text{score\_diff} \in \{0, +1\}$), in the defensive or middle third ($x < 66.7$)---captures $\mathbf{4,547}$ fouls ($9.5\%$ of all fouls), where the card rate jumps to $\mathbf{25.7\%}$ ($\mathbf{26.8\%}$ across the $2,069$ fouls committed while protecting a one-goal lead) versus $\mathbf{14.2\%}$ outside the window. Within these $4,547$ situational fouls, a club's mean foul $x$-position correlates with its situational card rate at $r = \mathbf{-0.268}$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.443, -0.074], p = 0.0076$). However, because clubs commit a median of only $45.5$ such fouls per season ($22.4\%$ variance reliability) and their situational foul share spans only $4.5\%$ to $14.8\%$, club situational share correlates with full-season card rate at $r = \mathbf{+0.053}$ ($[-0.147, +0.249], R^2 = \mathbf{0.3\%}$), and full position $\times$ minute $\times$ score-state interactions explain only $2.5\%$ of the between-club SD ($R^2 = 4.2\%$).

To determine how much of a single season's adjusted booking index reflects a repeatable club trait versus Poisson sampling noise, we constructed $2,528$ consecutive same-division club-season pairs ($\ge 25$ matches per season) across all $11$ leagues in our $58,013$-match archive.

\subsection{Persistence, Poisson Null, and Variance Decomposition}
Across the $2,528$ consecutive pairs, a club's situation- and era-adjusted booking index in season $t$ correlates with its index in season $t+1$ at $r = \mathbf{+0.324}$ ($R^2 = 10.5\%, p = 6.5\times 10^{-63}$), and is positive in $\mathbf{11}$ of $\mathbf{11}$ leagues (ranging from $+0.172$ in Scotland to $+0.427$ in Italy; Figure~\ref{fig:fig4}(a) and Table~\ref{tab:league_universe}). Resampling each club-season's yellow cards $2,000$ times from $\text{Poisson}(\text{expected}_{i,s})$ while holding fixture lists and expectations fixed produces a matched null with $\bar{r} = -0.0004$, $95\text{th}$ percentile $+0.032$, and a maximum across all $2,000$ draws of $\mathbf{+0.056}$ ($p < 0.0005$).

Decomposing the observed cross-sectional variance $\sigma^2_{\text{obs}} = 0.01735$ into expected Poisson sampling variance $\sigma^2_{\text{Poisson}} = E[Y / E[Y]^2] = 0.01473$ and true club heterogeneity $\sigma^2_{\text{true}} = \sigma^2_{\text{obs}} - \sigma^2_{\text{Poisson}} = 0.00262$ reveals that only $\mathbf{15.1\%}$ of the spread in single-season booking indices is a true club property ($\sigma_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{0.051}$), while $\mathbf{84.9\%}$ is the random Poisson noise of one season's cards.

\subsection{Out-of-Sample Shrinkage Validation}
Because single-season reliability is $0.151$, ranking clubs on one season of unshrunk ratios is dominated by estimation error. Across $2,432$ consecutive club-season pairs ($\ge 30$ matches), we evaluated three forecasts of a club's season-$t+1$ booking rate: (i) the club's own season-$t$ raw ratio, (ii) the league mean (ignoring the club entirely), and (iii) an Empirical-Bayes Beta-Binomial shrunken estimator $\hat{\theta}_i = \frac{n_i}{n_i + n_0} r_i + \frac{n_0}{n_i + n_0} \bar{r}_L$ \citep{efron1975stein,brown2008season}. Out-of-sample RMSE is $0.0267$ for the club's raw ratio, $0.0236$ for the league mean, and $0.0232$ for the shrunken estimate: \textbf{ignoring the club and predicting the league mean beats the club's own one-season ratio by $11.6\%$}, and \textbf{Empirical-Bayes shrinkage beats the raw ratio by $13.3\%$}.

\subsection{Pan-European Early-Season Outlier Scan and Historical Reversion}
The danger of unshrunk ratios is most acute in early-season leaderboards. Scanning all $\mathbf{194}$ active clubs across all $\mathbf{11}$ European leagues in the opening weeks of the $2026\text{--}27$ season (snapshot \texttt{2026-W36}, $255$ matches) reveals extreme raw fouls-per-yellow ratios across the continent: five clubs (FC K\"oln, Lorient, Schalke~04, Como, and Borussia M\"onchengladbach) stood at $\infty$ fouls/yellow ($0$ cards), followed by FC Porto (\texttt{P1}, $58.0$ fouls/yellow; $58$ fouls, $1$ yellow in $4$ matches, adjusted index $0.120$, BH $p = 0.081$), Zulte Waregem (\texttt{B1}, $45.0$; $45$ fouls, $1$ yellow in $4$ matches), PAOK (\texttt{G1}, $39.0$), Barcelona (\texttt{SP1}, $29.0$), and Paris FC (\texttt{F1}, $21.0$). Yet under within-league Beta-Binomial Empirical-Bayes shrinkage (\texttt{scripts/scan\_outliers.py}), all ten raw outliers shrink back to $6.16\text{--}10.60$ fouls/yellow with $95\%$ credible intervals overlapping their respective league means ($5.98\text{--}8.11$), and only $\mathbf{1}$ of $\mathbf{194}$ European clubs crosses the $P(\theta_i < \bar{r}_L) > 0.95$ posterior tail---far \emph{fewer} than the $0.05 \times 194 = \mathbf{9.7}$ clubs expected by chance alone.

To establish the historical base rate across Europe, we severed all $\mathbf{3,100}$ completed team-seasons in our $11$-league archive after match~$4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:fig4}(b)). Across all $3,100$ team-seasons, the four-match opening index correlates with the rest-of-season index (matches $5+$) at only $r = \mathbf{+0.118}$. The most lenient $1\%$ of four-match openings ($\le 0.292$, $31$ team-seasons spanning $8$ leagues, including Feyenoord $2023\text{--}24$ [$30$ fouls, $0$ yellows $\to 0.824$ rest-of-season], Norwich $2012\text{--}13$ [$48$ fouls, $0$ yellows $\to 1.173$], Celtic $2012\text{--}13$ [$32$ fouls, $0$ yellows $\to 0.965$], Galatasaray $2024\text{--}25$ [$54$ fouls, $1$ yellow $\to 1.083$], Las Palmas $2024\text{--}25$ [$47$ fouls, $1$ yellow $\to 1.459$], Tottenham $2014\text{--}15$ [$49$ fouls, $1$ yellow $\to 1.258$], Ajax $2018\text{--}19$ [$47$ fouls, $1$ yellow $\to 0.894$], Dortmund $2017\text{--}18$ [$49$ fouls, $1$ yellow $\to 0.921$], Chelsea $2004\text{--}05$ [$56$ fouls, $1$ yellow $\to 1.166$], and Bayern Munich $2021\text{--}22$ [$35$ fouls, $1$ yellow $\to 0.880$]) finished with a rest-of-season median index of $\mathbf{0.965}$ and a minimum of $\mathbf{0.663}$ (Leicester $2019\text{--}20$)---none approaching the $25$-year full-season archive floor (Celtic $2024\text{--}25$ at $0.548$).

Prospective tracking through snapshot \texttt{2026-W42} ($665$ matches across all $11$ leagues) confirmed this reversion in real time: every zero-yellow opening disappeared, Zulte Waregem regressed from $45.0$ to $11.5$ fouls/yellow ($8$ yellows in $92$ fouls), PAOK regressed from $39.0$ to $11.9$ ($8$ yellows in $95$ fouls), and FC Porto---for whom a sealed prospective protocol was registered on 30 August 2026---received $7$ yellow cards across $53$ fouls in its subsequent four matches, dropping from $58.0$ to $13.9$ fouls/yellow ($7.73$ shrunken vs.\ league mean $5.85$), while across all $194$ European clubs still only $1$ club exceeded the $0.95$ posterior threshold versus $9.7$ expected by chance.

\section{Conclusion}

Across $58,013$ matches, $246$ clubs in $11$ European leagues, $35,844$ referee-tracked team-matches, and $47,955$ spatio-temporally coded fouls, apparent club-level disciplinary anomalies dissolve under proper statistical controls. Raw cards-per-foul ratios make nearly every European domestic champion appear leniently officiated ($r = -0.371$) because they confound club identity with pre-match win expectation ($1.098\times$ vs.\ $0.856\times$), non-foul conduct intercepts ($11.1\%\text{--}38.8\%$ league-mean card share), and fixture-level competitive intensity shared with the opponent ($r = +0.283$). Once affine intercepts and fixture quality are netted out, club strength becomes orthogonal to booking rates ($r = +0.095, p = 0.139$) and Europe's perennial champions collapse onto parity ($\approx 1.00$), while leave-one-club-out referee assignment across $80$ clubs in England, Scotland, and Portugal shifts club indices by an RMS of just $0.0085$. At the event level, the $3.5$-fold spatial card gradient goes unexploited between clubs because team foul distributions compress into $[48.2, 58.0]$, and at the season level, $84.9\%$ of cross-sectional variance is Poisson noise, making Empirical-Bayes shrinkage mandatory before any disciplinary ratio is interpreted.
